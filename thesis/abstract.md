Safe UAV navigation in cluttered environments requires anticipating collisions with moving obstacles while avoiding local deadlocks around large and concave structures.
However, existing reinforcement-learning-based policies are commonly trained using instantaneous distance-based safety signals, which can lead to late avoidance maneuvers and myopic decisions. This paper presents Learning-based Predictive Navigation (LP-Nav), a reinforcement learning framework for UAV navigation built on Proximal Policy Optimization. 
We introduce a finite-horizon three-dimensional velocity-obstacle reward that incorporates relative motion and obstacle geometry to encourage proactive avoidance of dynamic obstacles. 
Moreover, an action-conditioned multi-horizon auxiliary objective predicts task-relevant rollout outcomes following each observation-action pair, regularizing the shared representation with future safety and navigability signals and thereby improving long-horizon detouring and mitigating local deadlocks around large obstacles. 
Simulation experiments demonstrate that, compared with NavRL, LP-Nav increases the dynamic-obstacle navigation success rate from 74.3% to 84.6%, an improvement of 10.3 percentage points, while reducing deadlocks and increasing post-deadlock recovery in large-obstacle environments. Zero-shot real-world flights further demonstrate deployment feasibility without fine-tuning. 
These results show that predictive representation learning and geometry-aware 3D velocity-obstacle shaping can effectively enhance UAV navigation in complex environments without additional online prediction or planning.
