Safe UAV navigation in cluttered environments requires anticipating collisions with moving obstacles while avoiding local deadlocks around large and concave structures.
However, existing reinforcement-learning-based policies are commonly trained using instantaneous distance-based safety signals, which can lead to late avoidance maneuvers and myopic decisions. This paper presents Learning-based Predictive Navigation (LP-Nav), a reinforcement learning framework for UAV navigation built on Proximal Policy Optimization.
We introduce a finite-horizon, time-to-collision-aware three-dimensional velocity-obstacle reward that incorporates relative motion and obstacle geometry to encourage proactive avoidance of dynamic obstacles.
Moreover, an action-conditioned multi-horizon auxiliary objective provides multi-step near-term outcome supervision by predicting task-relevant rollout outcomes following each observation-action pair. Its losses regularize the observation encoder shared by the actor and critic with semantically separated collision, deadlock, clearance, and progress signals.
Simulation experiments demonstrate that, compared with NavRL, LP-Nav increases the dynamic-obstacle navigation success rate from 74.3% to 84.6%, an improvement of 10.3 percentage points. In large-obstacle environments, the complete LP-Nav framework reduces deadlocks and achieves a higher post-deadlock escape rate. Zero-shot real-world flights further demonstrate deployment feasibility without fine-tuning.
Overall, LP-Nav achieves these improvements without additional online prediction or planning.
